The Story

The Future the Telephone Forgot

In 1947, Harold "Hal" Pemberton envisioned a telephone that could do more than connect voices. His Audio Enrichment System would bring news, stories, and entertainment through the phone already in your living room.

AT&T rejected the idea. So Pemberton quit and founded the Beacon Audio Works in a converted textile mill in Beacon Falls, Connecticut.

"Why just talk when you can listen? The telephone is already in 40 million American homes. Let's give it a voice." — Harold Pemberton, 1947

For fifteen years, they produced beautiful handcrafted "Listening Stations." Then, in 1962, facing pressure from Bell System's legal department, the Beacon Audio Works closed its doors.

Sixty-two years later, the archive was rediscovered. HelloHistory is the realization of Pemberton's original vision—finally answered.
